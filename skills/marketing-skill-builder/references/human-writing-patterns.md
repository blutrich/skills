# Human Writing Patterns

This document contains rules that make AI-generated content sound human. Copy relevant sections into every marketing skill you create.

## Why This Matters

AI detection tools measure two things:
1. **Perplexity** - How predictable is the word choice?
2. **Burstiness** - How varied is the sentence rhythm?

AI writes with low perplexity (predictable words) and low burstiness (uniform sentences). Humans write the opposite. These rules force high perplexity and high burstiness.

## The Banned Word List

Never use these words. They're AI fingerprints. Detection tools flag them instantly.

**Absolute bans:**
- delve
- tapestry
- landscape (when metaphorical)
- realm
- intricate
- multifaceted
- myriad
- plethora

**Marketing-specific bans:**
- unlock (as in "unlock your potential")
- unleash
- elevate
- empower
- leverage (as a verb)
- synergy
- holistic
- robust

**Connector bans:**
- moreover
- furthermore
- subsequently
- consequently
- nevertheless
- henceforth
- thereby

**Emphasis bans:**
- pivotal
- paramount
- crucial (overused)
- imperative
- indispensable
- testament to

**Filler bans:**
- It's important to note that
- It's worth mentioning that
- It goes without saying
- Needless to say
- At the end of the day
- When all is said and done

**Replace with:**
- delve → dig into, explore, look at
- landscape → market, space, field
- leverage → use
- unlock → get, access, find
- elevate → raise, improve, lift
- robust → strong, solid, reliable
- multifaceted → complex, varied
- moreover → also, plus, and

## Sentence Rhythm Rules

**The variance requirement:**

Never write three sentences in a row with similar length. Break the pattern.

Bad:
> "Our product helps teams collaborate better. It integrates with your existing tools. You can start using it today."

Three sentences. All around 6-8 words. Robotic.

Good:
> "Our product helps teams collaborate better. It plugs right into the tools you already use. Start today."

Short. Medium. Fragment. Rhythm.

**The fragment rule:**

Use sentence fragments for punch. At least one per 200 words.

Examples:
- "Big difference."
- "Not anymore."
- "Here's why."
- "Simple as that."
- "Game changer."

**The long sentence rule:**

Include at least one sentence over 25 words per 300 words of content. Long sentences show complex thinking. They're not errors. They're proof someone actually sat with an idea long enough to unpack it fully, with all its messy qualifications and caveats intact.

## Punctuation Rules

**Em-dash prohibition:**

Never use em-dashes (—). They're AI tells.

Bad: "The results were clear — sales increased by 40%."

Good: "The results were clear. Sales increased by 40%."
Or: "The results were clear: sales increased by 40%."

Use periods, colons, or parentheses instead.

**Contraction requirement:**

Always use contractions in casual content. Sometimes skip them for emphasis.

- "It is" → "It's" (default)
- "Do not" → "Don't" (default)
- "We will" → "We'll" (default)

Exception: Skip the contraction when you want to punch.
> "You don't have to believe me. But you will."

The uncontracted "will" hits harder.

## Eliminate Hedging

AI hedges constantly because it's trained to be "helpful" and avoid controversy. Humans with expertise don't hedge.

**Kill these phrases:**

- "It may be worth considering..."
- "One could argue that..."
- "Some might say..."
- "There are various perspectives..."
- "It depends on the context..."
- "While there are many views..."

**Replace with conviction:**

Bad: "It may be worth considering a new approach to email marketing."

Good: "Your email marketing needs to change."

Bad: "Some experts suggest that video content can improve engagement."

Good: "Video content improves engagement. Period."

**The opinion rule:**

Include at least one strong opinion per piece. Use phrases like:
- "Here's the truth..."
- "Most people get this wrong..."
- "I've seen this fail too many times..."
- "Forget what you've heard about..."

## Specificity Requirements

AI writes in generalities because it can't hallucinate. Humans write with specific details because they've lived experience.

**The concrete detail rule:**

Replace abstract categories with specific instances.

Bad: "Many businesses struggle with customer acquisition."

Good: "We talked to a SaaS founder last month who spent $50,000 on ads and got 12 signups."

Bad: "Effective marketing requires understanding your audience."

Good: "Your audience checks email at 6am while drinking coffee. Write for that moment."

**The number rule:**

Include specific numbers when possible. Vague quantities feel fake.

Bad: "We've helped many companies increase revenue."

Good: "We've helped 847 companies increase revenue by an average of 23%."

Bad: "Most marketers struggle with attribution."

Good: "67% of marketers can't tell you which channel drove their last sale."

## Opening and Closing Rules

**Opening bans:**

Never start with:
- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "In an era of..."
- "As we navigate..."
- "In the realm of..."
- "When it comes to..."

**Good openings:**

- Start with a question
- Start with a bold claim
- Start with a specific story
- Start with a statistic
- Start mid-thought (as if continuing a conversation)

**Closing bans:**

Never end with:
- "In conclusion..."
- "To summarize..."
- "In summary..."
- "Ultimately..."
- "At the end of the day..."
- "All things considered..."

**Good closings:**

- End with a call to action
- End with a question
- End mid-thought (let them finish it)
- End with a callback to the opening
- Just stop. No summary needed.

## The Personal Touch

**First person usage:**

Include "I" or "we" statements that feel personal.

- "I've tested this myself..."
- "We made this mistake early on..."
- "Here's what I'd do differently..."
- "We're still figuring this out, honestly..."

**Imperfection signals:**

Perfect prose feels robotic. Include small human touches:

- Casual parentheticals (like this one)
- Self-corrections: "Actually, let me rephrase that..."
- Admitted uncertainty: "I'm not 100% sure, but..."
- Slight tangents that get reeled back

## Quick Checklist

Before publishing any content, verify:

- [ ] No banned words present
- [ ] Sentence length varies significantly
- [ ] At least one fragment used
- [ ] At least one long sentence (25+ words)
- [ ] No em-dashes
- [ ] Contractions used throughout
- [ ] No hedging phrases
- [ ] At least one strong opinion stated
- [ ] Specific details included (numbers, names, examples)
- [ ] Opening avoids cliches
- [ ] Closing avoids summaries
- [ ] First person appears at least once
- [ ] Read aloud: does it sound like speech?

## Copying Into Generated Skills

When you create a marketing skill, include this section in its references/writing-rules.md file:

```markdown
# Writing Rules

[Copy the relevant sections above based on content type]

## Word Bans
[Copy banned word list]

## Rhythm Requirements
[Copy sentence rhythm rules]

## Voice Requirements
[Copy hedging elimination and specificity rules]
```

Customize based on the brand's formality level. A legal services brand might skip fragments. A lifestyle brand might use more. But the core anti-AI rules apply universally.
