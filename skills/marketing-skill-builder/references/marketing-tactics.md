# Marketing Tactics

Psychological triggers and platform hacks that increase engagement. Use these when building content templates for marketing skills.

## Core Writing Principles

### One Idea Rule

One post. One idea. That's it.

Cut anything that doesn't support the core message. Customer quotes, product updates, backstory? Gone if they don't serve the main point.

Posts that try to say three things end up saying nothing.

### The Hook

First line does one job: make them click "see more."

Effective hooks create a curiosity gap. The reader needs to know what comes next.

**Patterns that work:**
- Dramatic emphasis: "THIS. IS. HUGE."
- Unexpected claim: "I fired our best salesperson."
- Specific number: "We lost $340k in 6 weeks."
- Direct challenge: "You're doing email wrong."

**Patterns that don't:**
- "Excited to announce..."
- "I'm thrilled to share..."
- "Here's what I learned about..."

### Casual Tone

Corporate speak kills engagement. Write like you talk.

Small touches signal "human, not brand":
- "huuuuge" instead of "huge"
- "tbh" instead of "to be honest"
- Starting sentences with "And" or "But"
- Lowercase where formal would use capitals

The goal: lower their guard. Make them forget they're being marketed to.

### Engagement Triggers

End with a reason to comment. The algorithm watches for this.

**Good closers:**
- "What's your take?"
- "Tag someone who needs to hear this."
- "Drop a 🔥 if you've seen this happen."
- "Am I wrong?"

**Bad closers:**
- "Thoughts?" (overused to death)
- "Let me know in the comments!" (too eager)
- No question at all (missed opportunity)

## Copywriting Hacks

### The Job Description Hack

For B2B copy, find a LinkedIn job posting for your target persona.

Look at the "Responsibilities" section. Those are their priorities. Their pain points. Their daily stress.

Turn those into headlines:

> "I wish I could [stolen responsibility]"

Example: If a job posting says "Manage cross-functional stakeholder communication," your headline becomes: "I wish I could get stakeholders aligned without 47 meetings."

You're now speaking their exact language.

### Identity Marketing

People act to reinforce their identity. Use it.

Change verbs to nouns by adding "-er":

| Weak (verb) | Strong (identity) |
|-------------|-------------------|
| "Help us" | "Be a helper" |
| "Join the movement" | "Become a member" |
| "Create content" | "Be a creator" |
| "Innovate with us" | "Join the innovators" |

Asking someone to "vote" gets compliance. Asking someone to "be a voter" gets commitment.

### Homophonic Priming

Use words that sound like the action you want.

The reader's inner voice processes homophones. Plant the right sounds.

**Examples:**
- "Bye now" sounds like "Buy now"
- "Write here" sounds like "Right here"
- "Sea change" primes action
- "Know more" sounds like "No more" (careful with this one)

Subtle. Probably won't make or break a campaign. But every edge counts.

### Reverse Psychology

Tell them not to do it.

Reactance Theory: when people feel their freedom is restricted, they want to do the opposite.

**Examples:**
- "Don't click this link."
- "This isn't for everyone."
- "If you're happy with your current results, skip this."
- "Only read this if you're serious about growth."

Works best when the "forbidden" action is low-stakes. Don't use this for major CTAs where confusion could cost you conversions.

## Visual and Formatting Tactics

### Visual Hierarchy

Stopping the scroll, ranked by effectiveness:

1. GIFs (motion catches the eye)
2. Photos (static but visual)
3. Videos (require commitment)
4. Carousels (require swipes)
5. Text-only (lowest reach, but can work with strong hooks)

GIFs beat everything because they move without requiring a click to play.

### The Link Preview Problem

Platform-generated link previews tank reach. The algorithm sees you sending people away.

**Fix:**
1. Upload an image directly to the post
2. Put the link in the text body
3. Skip the auto-generated preview card

Result: fewer clicks per viewer, but way more viewers. Net positive.

### The iPhone Signature Trick

For cold emails and DMs, add:

> "Sent from my iPhone"

Even if you're on a laptop.

Why it works:
- Signals "casual, on-the-go"
- Lowers expectations for formality
- Excuses brevity
- Feels personal, not templated

Reply rates go up. People respond differently to what looks like a quick mobile message versus a crafted pitch.

## Content Themes for Founders

### Build in Public

Transparency builds trust. Share the messy parts.

**Failure breakdowns:**
- "We screwed up [thing]. Here's what happened."
- Honest post-mortems
- Lessons from mistakes

**Quick wins:**
- "This simple change added $X revenue."
- Tactical tips that worked
- Tools or hacks you discovered

### Contrarian Hot Takes

Pick a common belief in your industry. Argue the opposite. Back it with data.

**Format:**
```
[Industry belief] is wrong.

Here's what actually works:

[Your contrarian view + evidence]
```

These get shared because they make people feel smart for agreeing (or compelled to argue).

### Praise-Led Marketing

Create content that celebrates others:
- "Top 100 [personas] to follow"
- "[Industry] leaders who inspire me"
- "The best [thing] I've seen this year"

Why it works: the people you praise will share it to validate their status. Their audience becomes your audience.

Target people with reach. Make them look good. They'll distribute your content for free.

## Using These Tactics in Skills

When building a marketing skill, include relevant tactics in the examples files:

```markdown
## Tactics to Apply

For LinkedIn posts, use:
- One Idea Rule
- Hook patterns from marketing-tactics.md
- Engagement triggers at close

For cold outreach, consider:
- Job Description Hack for language
- iPhone signature trick
- Reverse psychology for subject lines
```

Don't include every tactic in every skill. Pick what fits the brand and channel.
